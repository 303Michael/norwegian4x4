About the Norwegian 4×4 Protocol
What is it?
Four intervals of 4 minutes at 85–95% of your max heart rate, with 3 minutes of active recovery between each. Total workout time is around 40 minutes including warm-up.
Where it comes from
Developed by Professor Jan Helgerud and colleagues at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU). A landmark 2007 study showed 4×4 interval training improved VO₂max significantly more than moderate continuous exercise over the same period.
How often
Two to three sessions per week is the evidence-based recommendation. More than three sessions offers diminishing returns and increases injury risk. Most people see meaningful VO₂max gains within 8 weeks.
During recovery
Keep moving — walk, jog slowly, or pedal easy. Active recovery clears lactate faster than standing still and prepares you better for the next interval. Your HR should drop below 70% by the end of the 3 minutes.
Pacing tip
If you can't reach 85% HR in the final interval, you started too easy. If you can't hold 85% by the end of each interval, you started too hard. The last interval should feel like the hardest thing you've done all week.
Works on any cardio machine
Treadmill, bike, rower, ski erg — the protocol is equipment-agnostic. Running outdoors on a hill works well. The key is sustained intensity, not the modality.
Helgerud J, et al. "Aerobic high-intensity intervals improve VO₂max more than moderate training." Med Sci Sports Exerc, 2007. Official guidance from the NTNU Cardiac Exercise Research Group (CERG).
